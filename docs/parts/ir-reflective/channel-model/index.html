The flicker is an upper bound, not a figure. The converter figure comes from the MCP3008 datasheet, the reference residue from the rail difference the design allows itself to leave after the correction. The modulation depth behind the flicker is assumed, and the stock board argues it down: a 3 ms period gives no trouble in ordinary rooms, which this depth would not allow. Inside one phase

How much of the sensor's full swing the worst channel gets, and when the block starts reading. The bar is the read performance, lit minus dark as a share of the full swing, and every other figure in the tool scales with it. Both curves start where the last phase left them, so a phase too short for the pull-down keeps them near the middle, and left of the red line the lit curve runs below the dark one, where a reading reports backwards. The shaded band is the read block, and the further left it starts, the longer that block is.

Channel by channel

What every channel would read with a ball on it, and what it reads over a clear track, in the order they are read. The distance between the two lines is all the decision has. Green where that distance is wide enough for at most one false report a year, amber down to one a day, red below. The mark under each ball reading is the release threshold; it turns red where it comes within 2 σ of the clear track, because a ball that leaves would then never be released.
